## Summary

The `GetStatementLineQuery` endpoint regressed from ~300 ms to 33–73 s between the two log windows. The cause is almost certainly the migration `20260914_2100_ReindexStatementLines` that ran during the 4.7.0 deployment. Two comments in the ticket are contradicted by the log evidence.

---

## Quantified Regression

| Metric | Before (4.6.3, 09-13) | After (4.7.0, 09-15) |
|---|---|---|
| `GetStatementLineQuery` range | 281–319 ms | 33,757–72,974 ms |
| `GetStatementLineQuery` average | ~300 ms | ~51,800 ms (14 samples) |
| Slowdown factor | — | **~112× (minimum) to ~243× (maximum)** |
| Requests exceeding 10 s threshold | 0 of 10 | 14 of 14 |
| ThreadPool starvation | None | 1 event: 31 items queued, 0 threads for 4,200 ms |

The degradation is **progressive** within the "after" window: early calls sit around 35 s and later calls climb to ~73 s, suggesting queueing or backpressure compounds over time rather than a single fixed cost.

---

## What Changed

- **`GetStatementLineQuery`** — the only query that regressed. Every single sample in the "after" window exceeds the 10 s warning threshold.
- **A new migration ran at deployment:** `Applied 1 pending migration: 20260914_2100_ReindexStatementLines` at 2026-09-14 21:05:36 UTC. The name directly references the table behind the degraded query.
- **Pod identity changed** (`6b9f4d7c85` → `7d4a2f9c61`), consistent with a rolling deployment.
- **ThreadPool starvation** appeared, a secondary symptom of requests occupying threads for 30–70 s each.

## What Did Not Change

- **`GetAccountSummaryQuery`** — 2,987–3,402 ms in both windows. No regression.
- **`ExportStatementCommand`** — 1,840 ms vs 1,902 ms. Essentially identical.
- **Health check / DB latency** — 6 ms vs 9 ms. Both healthy.
- **Overall service liveness** — still reporting healthy in both windows.

## What the Logs Cannot Establish

1. **Causation of the reindex.** The migration name is strongly suggestive, but the logs do not show what the migration did (dropped an index? replaced one? was it still running at the time of the window?). Correlation is high; proof is not in these files.
2. **Whether the reindex completed successfully.** There is no log line confirming completion or reporting its duration.
3. **Why the degradation is progressive (35 s → 73 s).** Possible explanations — connection-pool exhaustion, lock contention from a still-running migration, growing queue depth — are all consistent with these logs but none is distinguishable from them.
4. **Whether the issue is persistent or transient.** The "after" window is only five minutes. It is unknown whether the query recovers once any background work finishes.
5. **The customer-facing experience.** Browser timeouts and "over a minute" complaints come from the ticket description, not from these logs. The logs confirm server-side latency but not what the user actually sees (e.g., whether a proxy or load balancer adds its own timeout).
6. **The intended purpose of the reindex.** It is unclear whether it was meant to *improve* performance (and failed), to restructure the table, or for some other reason.
