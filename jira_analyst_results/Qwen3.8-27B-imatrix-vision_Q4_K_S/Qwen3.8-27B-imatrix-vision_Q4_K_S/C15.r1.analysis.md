# Analysis of BANK-471

## 1. Summary

Customers stopped receiving payment-receipt (and other) email notifications at roughly 14:00 on 2026-09-15. The production log shows every outbound email is failing with **SMTP 535 – "Authentication unsuccessful, basic authentication is disabled"** against `smtp.example.com:587`. The notifications pod itself is up; it is the SMTP server that is rejecting the service's credentials. The outbox backlog has grown from 1 412 to 2 089 messages in the ~37 minutes the log covers, with no backoff or dead-letter path.

## 2. What the log actually shows (key lines)

- **18:02:41–42 – Data Protection warnings (real, but not the cause):**
  > `Storing keys in a directory '/app/.aspnet/DataProtection-Keys' that may not be persisted outside of the container.`
  > `No XML encryptor configured. Key {3c8e5a19-…} may be persisted to storage in unencrypted form.`

- **18:02:48 – First SMTP failure (the actual error):**
  > `System.Net.Mail.SmtpException: The SMTP server requires a secure connection or the client was not authenticated. The server response was: 5.7.139 Authentication unsuccessful, basic authentication is disabled.`
  > `SMTP status code: 535`

  This exact error repeats every 5 s for customer 41082, then for customer 63340 at 18:38, and for a `statement-ready` notification (customer 55617) at 18:28. **All notification types are affected, not just payment receipts.**

- **18:05:00 – SMTP config logged in plain text:**
  > `SMTP host smtp.example.com:587, user svc-notify@example.com, StartTls=True.`

  The credentials are visible in the log unencrypted, which means they are **not** wrapped by ASP.NET Data Protection. This is directly relevant to the diagnosis question below.

- **18:03:08 – Queue behaviour:**
  > `Outbox item 991204 has reached 5 delivery attempts and stays at the head of the queue. Retry interval is fixed at 5s; there is no backoff and no dead-letter path.`

- **18:21:09 – Health check:**
  > `Health check /healthz: Healthy (db 8ms, nats 3ms, smtp skipped).`

  SMTP is explicitly excluded from the health check, so the pod reported "Healthy" while every email was failing.

- **NATS blip at 18:07** (lost → reconnected after 1 attempt) – minor, self-recovered, unrelated to the SMTP issue.

## 3. Does the log support Marcus's explanation?

**No.** Marcus's comment states:

> "The service is storing its data protection keys in a directory that is not persisted and there is no XML encryptor configured, so the keys are effectively junk. That is why the notifications are failing."

The Data Protection warnings are genuine and should be addressed, but they do **not** explain the SMTP 535 error:

- The failure is an **SMTP server-side authentication rejection** ("basic authentication is disabled"), not a local decryption failure. If Data Protection had lost its keys and the service couldn't decrypt stored credentials, you would expect a different exception (e.g. a `CryptographicException` or a 401/476 from the SMTP server), not the specific "basic auth is disabled" message.
- The SMTP credentials are logged in **plain text** at 18:05:00, confirming they are not Data-Protection-encrypted. The key-loss scenario therefore does not apply to these credentials.
- The pod was already restarted at 18:02; the same 535 errors continue. If the problem were lost keys, a restart would at least regenerate them (producing a *different* failure mode – inability to decrypt previously-protected state). Instead the error is identical and clearly originates from the SMTP server.

**Likely actual root cause (inference):** The SMTP server at `smtp.example.com:587` was reconfigured (or a credential was rotated) so that it no longer accepts basic/SMTP-AUTH. The service is still attempting basic auth with `svc-notify@example.com`. This is a server-side or credential change, not a notifications-code issue. Without a linked ticket or changelog entry for the SMTP infrastructure, the exact trigger is not in this ticket.

## 6. Risks and gaps

- **Backlog is growing in real time.** From 1 412 → 2 089 in ~37 min ≈ +18 msg/min. By the end of the business day the queue could exceed 10 000 messages. With no backoff or dead-letter, a single stuck item (991204) pins the queue head and blocks all subsequent items even after the SMTP fix.
- **Scope is wider than the ticket title implies.** The `statement-ready` failure at 18:28:33 shows all email notification types are down, not just payment receipts. The ticket title and description only mention payment receipts.
- **Health check is misleading.** `smtp skipped` means the pod reports Healthy while 100 % of emails are failing. Monitoring/alerting did not catch this; the outage was discovered via customer calls.
- **Data Protection issue is a real but separate defect.** Keys in a non-persistent path with no XML encryptor means any previously-protected state (cookies, anti-forgery tokens, possibly other encrypted fields) is lost on every pod restart. This should be a **separate ticket** (e.g. "Persist Data Protection keys / configure XML encryptor") and should not be conflated with the current SMTP outage.
- **Log gap.** The previous container's log was not captured. If the failure started at 14:00, the logs between 14:00 and 18:02 are lost, making it harder to identify exactly when and why the SMTP server started rejecting.
- **Credential in plain text in logs.** `svc-notify@example.com` and the SMTP host are written to stdout. This is a minor security hygiene issue.
- **Conflicting framing in comments.** Marcus calls the cause "obvious" and "fairly clear cut" and directs Platform to fix "key storage." Priya, who performed the restart, is still uncertain ("Should we just restart it again?"). The ticket has no resolution because the two comments are pointing at different problems.

## 8. Direct answers to the specific questions asked

**What does the log actually show?**
Every outbound email is failing with SMTP 535 ("Authentication unsuccessful, basic authentication is disabled") against `smtp.example.com:587`. The outbox is growing with no backoff or dead-letter. The pod itself is healthy (NATS connected, DB reachable). The Data Protection warnings are present but are a separate concern. The health check explicitly skips SMTP.

**Does it support the reporter's explanation?**
No. The Data Protection warnings are real but do not cause an SMTP 535 authentication rejection. The credentials are logged in plain text, proving they are not Data-Protection-encrypted. The root cause is a server-side change on the SMTP relay (basic auth disabled or credentials rotated), not a key-storage problem in the notifications container.

**What should happen next?**
Immediately engage the Platform / Mail-Relay team to confirm the SMTP server's current auth policy and supply valid credentials or a new auth mechanism. Do **not** restart the pod again. After the fix, drain and monitor the growing outbox backlog. File separate tickets for the Data Protection key issue and the health-check gap. Update the ticket to reflect the true scope (all email notifications, not just payment receipts) and add tracking metadata.
