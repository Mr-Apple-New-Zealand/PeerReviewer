The two attached logs (same 5-minute clock window, before and after release) show a large, measurable regression in one specific query, `GetStatementLineQuery`, not in the query that a comment suggests is the cause.

## Log comparison

**GetStatementLineQuery — this is the regression:**
- Before: 281–319ms, consistently fast, appears many times with no warnings.
- After: no longer completes normally at all in this window; every instance is logged as a `[WRN] Long-running request`, ranging from 33,757ms up to 72,974ms.
- That is roughly a **100x to 240x** increase in response time for this specific query.
- The slowdown also **worsens across the window**: 35,171ms at 00:20:43 rising to 72,974ms by 00:24:34 — consistent with a backlog building rather than a flat, one-off delay.
- A new `[WRN] ThreadPool starvation detected: 31 work items queued, 0 threads available for 4200ms` appears only in the after log, at 00:24:11, in the middle of the worsening trend — this is a plausible mechanism for why later requests queue and get progressively slower, but the log doesn't say what is occupying the threads.

**GetAccountSummaryQuery — essentially unchanged:**
- Before: 2987–3341ms.
- After: 3188–3402ms.
- This is the query Ravi Kumar names as the likely cause ("It'll be the account summary query... always been slow"). The logs do **not** support that: its timing is flat across both windows. It may well be a long-standing slow query as he says, but nothing here shows it got worse or that it explains the new symptom (page timeouts).

**Unchanged / not implicated:**
- `ExportStatementCommand`: 1840ms before vs 1902ms after — no meaningful change.
- `/healthz` DB check: 6ms before vs 9ms after — both "Healthy," so basic DB connectivity/latency isn't obviously degraded.

**Notable difference not commented on by anyone:**
- The after log's startup line shows: `Applied 1 pending migration: 20260914_2100_ReindexStatementLines`, applied on release, timestamped just hours before the observed slowdown. This directly concerns statement lines and sits in tension with Tom Becker's comment that "nothing in 4.7.0 touched statements" — worth flagging as a conflict between that comment and the log evidence. (Inference: the migration is a plausible trigger — e.g. an incomplete reindex, lock contention, or a dropped/degraded index — but the log does not prove this; it only shows correlation in time.)

## What these logs cannot establish

- **Root cause.** The logs show *that* `GetStatementLineQuery` got slow and *that* thread pool starvation occurred, but not *why* — no DB query plan, lock/wait stats, index status, or CPU/memory metrics are present.
- **Causal link to the migration.** The `ReindexStatementLines` migration and the slowdown are close in time, but the logs only show correlation, not causation. It's equally possible the reindex is unrelated and something else in 4.7.0 (or infra) caused the starvation.
- **Whether this is one pod or all pods.** The before and after logs come from two different pod instances (`...w4lnp` vs `...xr6vh`), so it can't be ruled out that this is specific to one node/pod rather than universal across the fleet.
- **Scope/representativeness.** Only a 5-minute window is captured each side; there's no data on whether the slowdown is constant, intermittent, or tied to load (e.g., a concurrent batch job at midnight), and no confirmation the sampled requests are for comparable accounts/statement sizes.
- **User-facing timing.** The logs show backend request duration, not what the browser actually experienced; they're consistent with the reported symptom but don't independently confirm "over a minute or times out" at the UI layer.
- **Whether starvation is cause or symptom.** The one starvation warning appears mid-sequence; it's not established whether thread exhaustion is causing the long query times or is itself caused by something else queuing behind slow queries.
