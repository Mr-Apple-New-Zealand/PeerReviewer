Triage is a complex task that involves several steps:

1. **Data Collection**: The Triage system needs to collect data on the pending actions, such as the actions to be performed (e.g., "Add", "Update", "Delete", "Search", "Run"). This involves interacting with a database, which may have limitations such as insufficient data on the number of active branches or the types of actions that can be performed.

2. **Querying the Database**: The system queries the database to determine which actions are currently active. This involves selecting the appropriate actions based on predefined criteria, such as the "Active" branch ID, the "Quota" status, or the "Previous" actions.

3. **Processing the Results**: Once the active actions are identified, the Triage system processes the results, which may involve updating the associated tables, generating reports, or performing other actions based on the data.

4. **Handling Errors**: If the query results do not meet the predefined criteria, the system may throw an exception, indicating that the action cannot be performed due to insufficient data, inability to find a branch, or other operational constraints.

The Triage system is inherently error-prone due to its reliance on database interactions, which can be slow and error-prone, especially when dealing with large datasets and complex interactions. This is often mitigated through the use of caching mechanisms, batch processing, and efficient data processing pipelines.

The system logs the actions that can be performed, providing valuable insights into the operational efficiency and identifying potential bottlenecks. This helps in designing more effective and efficient workflows, reducing the need for manual intervention, and improving overall system reliability.

Triage does not allow for silent acceptance of actions that do not meet the predefined criteria; instead, it relies on the system's ability to process and analyze the data effectively. The system's design prioritizes data accuracy and robustness, ensuring that actions are taken with confidence in the event of failure.
